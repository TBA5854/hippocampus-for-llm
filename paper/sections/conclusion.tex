\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We made memory a part of the model rather than a harness around it: one memory
block that reads early and writes at the last block, addressed by a position-free
layer-0 key, turns a stateless transformer into a stateful one. On real language it
recalls facts stated once and queried out of context at \recall{100} through 32
facts and \recall{99} at 64, with matched controls at chance and the store
persisted across a process restart. Because the store is external to attention it
holds far more than the window---unbounded when grown like a KV cache, with a top-$1$
read holding \recall{100} on a single slot across a store of nearly a million slots---and
is retrieved sparsely, with sparse reading beating a dense scan. Reading \emph{and} writing at every layer of the one shared store, each layer
storing its own value, improves on read-early/write-late by $+16$ points. Ported
to this store, the three axes of DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash's shared-KV scheme each hold:
an index pool makes the read $O(1)$-compute, cross-layer reuse is free, and 4-bit
quantization is lossless. Placement ablations independently reveal the inter-layer
redundancy shared KV relies on, and decompose the classic fact-count cliff into a
single-site bottleneck that multi-layer placement escapes and a delayed-transition
bootstrapping cost that a larger training budget clears cold---the warm-start
curriculum reaching the same recall in about half the steps.

The result is a working, shared key--value episodic store inside the stack: it holds
$\gg$ the context window, is read in $O(1)$ slots at 4 bits per element, and answers
from nothing in the prompt. Its two open frontiers are, on present evidence,
\emph{compute} limits rather than limits of the method. Long-context reach is measured to a
\emph{million tokens}---top-$1$ at \recall{100} over a $990{,}473$-slot store on one slot---once
the store build is made memory-frugal (an in-place eval write); nothing in the model caps it,
the read is $O(1)$ per query, and the limit is now build wall-clock. Sweeping a passkey's
\emph{depth} in the filler finds \emph{no} lost-in-the-middle---recall is monotonic in
depth---but a write-order decay instead: a fact stored early persists while one stored late
into a saturated store is lost, which decomposes into store saturation and a fixed novelty
write-gate (unique keys partly lift it), not position. The store privileges what it saw
first, the opposite of attention's recency. And a single shared store
saturates near
\recall{45} at $128$ distinct facts, which is an \emph{addressing} problem of the kind
positional embeddings already solve in a KV cache: a symmetric positional/RoPE-style tag on
the query and the stored key---generalising the ordinal recency read that already
disambiguates same-key facts---should separate them without disturbing the exact-token match,
and several shared stores or more read heads on the store are the structural alternatives. We
leave both to future work with more compute. What remains beyond them is to make the measured
pool and 4-bit savings literal with hashed and int4 kernels, and to train with the
sparse/quantized read.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{External and retrieval memory.} MemGPT, generative agents, and
retrieval-augmented generation give language models long-term memory by storing and
retrieving text \emph{outside} the network and re-injecting it into the prompt.
This keeps the model stateless and ties recall to the context budget. Our store is
\emph{inside} the stack and addressed by the model's own representations, so recall
does not consume context and the model is stateful by construction.

\paragraph{KV-cache compression and shared KV.} A large line of work makes the
attention KV cache cheaper by sharing it \emph{across heads}: multi-query and
grouped-query attention share one (or a few) key/value projections among many query
heads, and multi-head latent attention (MLA, DeepSeek-V2/V3) caches a small latent
from which each head reconstructs its own KV. DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash's CSA2 shares KV
\emph{across layers}---a few anchor layers compute fresh KV and sparse selections,
the rest reuse them---and adds FP4 KV. We port CSA2's three axes (candidate pool,
cross-layer reuse, low-bit KV) to an \emph{external, persistent} episodic store
rather than the in-window cache, and find the same inter-layer redundancy from an
independent ablation of read/write placement.

\paragraph{Associative and hippocampal memory.} The read is a modern-Hopfield /
associative-memory retrieval with an annealed settling and a surrogate gradient.
The write/read/consolidate structure and the naming (dentate gyrus for pattern
separation, CA3 attractor retrieval, CA1 fusion gate, sleep-time consolidation)
follow hippocampal-indexing accounts of episodic memory; we find, however, that the
pattern-separation stage is incompatible with the exact-token key that makes the
system work, and leave it off.

\paragraph{Long-context transformers and memory-augmented models.} Recurrent-memory
and compressive transformers extend context by carrying or compressing past
activations---Transformer-XL caches the previous segment, and the Compressive
Transformer and Recurrent Memory Transformer carry a fixed-size recurrent state.
Closest to us is \emph{Memformer} (Wu et al., 2020), which adds an \emph{external}
key--value memory bank of fixed-size slots that the model reads and writes by
attention with a learned forgetting gate, achieving $O(1)$ inference space over
unbounded sequences and beating Transformer-XL and the Compressive Transformer on
WikiText-103. The difference is what the memory \emph{is}. These are lossy, learned
\emph{summaries} updated by attention with forgetting, so a fact mentioned once is
compressed into a shared recurrent state and can be overwritten; their aim is
language-model perplexity, not fact recall. Our store instead holds \emph{exact}
content addressed by a position-free token key, so a fact stated once is recoverable
verbatim arbitrarily later (measured to a million tokens); it \emph{grows} rather
than forgetting, is read in $O(1)$ \emph{slots} by an exact match rather than by
attention over the whole bank, and is \emph{persistent and detachable}---it survives
a process restart and swaps between models (the stateless-weights / stateful-store
thesis of \S\ref{sec:transplant})---none of which an in-sequence recurrent summary
provides. Memformer trades space for compression ($O(1)$ space, lossy); we trade
space for exactness (a growable store, $O(1)$ \emph{read}).
